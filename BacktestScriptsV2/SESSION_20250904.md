## 2. Work Completed

### PnL Analysis & Ad-hoc Calculation

1.  **Problem Identification:** We started by investigating why the final equity of a `SHORT` strategy was much lower than the expected `Initial Equity + Realized PnL`. I hypothesized that the discrepancy was due to dividend payments on short positions, which are a cash expense not captured in the trade PnL itself.

2.  **Ad-hoc Solution:** At your request, we implemented a non-intrusive solution in R. We created a new script, `AdhocCalcsScript.R`, containing a function to calculate the total dividend cost post-backtest by cross-referencing the results with the dividend data.

3.  **Bug Fix & Refinement:** You correctly identified a bug in the initial version of the ad-hoc script. I was summing the per-share dividend amount instead of the total nominal amount (`share_quantity * dividend_amount`). I corrected the logic in the function.

### Strategy Logic & Engine Architecture

1.  **`LONG_SHORT` Analysis:** When you began testing a `LONG_SHORT` strategy, you noticed only short trades were executing. We diagnosed this as a conflict between your "reversing system" signal design (where `long_exit` is identical to `short_entry`) and a core rule in the C++ engine that prevents an exit and a new entry on the same bar.

2.  **Best Practices:** We had a detailed discussion on best practices for signal design with this type of sequential backtesting engine. The key principle is that an `exit` signal should be a reason to "go flat," not a reason to "reverse." This requires decoupling the exit condition from the entry condition of the opposite trade direction.

### Strategy Refactoring

1.  **New Strategy Design:** Based on our discussion, you proposed an excellent new strategy: use a slower, 10-bar Ultimate Smoother for entries and a faster, 9-bar Ultimate Smoother for exits. This creates a natural "neutral zone" between trades.

2.  **Flexible Signal Generation:** To implement this, we needed a more flexible way to generate the indicator columns. We refactored `MainScript.R` to use your existing vector-based `ultimateSmoother()` function directly inside a `dplyr::mutate()` call. This allowed us to cleanly create two named columns, `smoother_10` and `smoother_9`.

3.  **Implementation:** Finally, I updated the `strategy_cfg` in `MainScript.R` to use the new columns, successfully implementing the 10-bar entry and 9-bar exit logic.
